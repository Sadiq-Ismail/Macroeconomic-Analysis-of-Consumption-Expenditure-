Macroeconomic Analysis of 
Consumption Expenditure 
1. Project Overview 
This project delivers a comprehensive, data-driven macroeconomic analysis of private final 
consumption expenditure in the domestic market across a seven-year timeline spanning 2004
05 to 2010-11. By executing high-fidelity data transformations on extensive national accounting 
datasets, the primary goal was to structurally isolate core consumption pillars, uncover long
term compound growth patterns, identify shifting consumer behavior, and visually communicate 
strategic market insights through an interactive business intelligence framework. 
2. Tools Used 
• Microsoft Excel: Deployed as the baseline engine for staging raw micro-records, parsing 
structural data matrices, cleaning null and nested entities, and verifying mathematical 
reconciliation via multi-axis pivot structures. 
• Power BI Desktop: Utilized to construct a dynamic star-schema data model, engineer 
calculated columns, formulate time-series metrics using DAX (Data Analysis Expressions), 
and engineer a production-ready analytical dashboard. 
3. Dataset 
• Source: National Accounts Historical Economic Statistics and Consumption Data Files 
(2004–2011). 
• Data Footprint: The database tracks historical records split across multiple granular 
columns including Item Name, Parent Group Name, and chronological spending values 
mapping out seven fiscal cycles: 2004-05, 2005-06, 2006-07, 2007-08, 2008-09, 2009-10, 
and 2010-11. 
4. Key Insights 
• Exponential Macro Expansion: Total verified private consumption expenditure grew 
aggressively, starting from an initial baseline of 1,925,592 in the 2004-05 cycle and 
compounding up to 4,359,792 by the 2010-11 cycle. This demonstrates a massive overall 
market scale expansion of 126.4%. 
• Category Market Dominance: The 'Food, Beverages & Tobacco' segment firmly 
established itself as the leading economic consumer engine. Standing at an unparalleled 
1,594,534 during the 2010-11 closing window, it captured a significant 36.6% market share 
of the total tracked national expenditure portfolio. 
• Top-Tier Micro Expenditure Drivers: The highest individual consumer items driving the 
2010-11 volume were led by 'Other Miscellaneous Services' at 440,503, followed closely by 
structural 'Gross Rent & Water Charges' at 425,533, and macro logistics via 'Purchase of 
Transport Services' marking 357,111. 
• Accelerated Dynamic Sectors: The 'Miscellaneous Goods & Services' group showed the 
highest momentum over the timeline, surging by a remarkable 285.3% increase from 
172,082 to 663,117.

• Step 1: Data Architecture Review: Open the main Excel workbook file to review raw
